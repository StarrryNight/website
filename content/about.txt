stack: SOLIDWORKS, Python, C, C#, 3D Printing, Machine Learning

I build autonomous systems — mostly the software that lets a machine work out where it is, what is around it, and what to do next. My work tends to sit between machine learning and control: state estimation for a RoboCup soccer team, transfer learning on genomic sequence data, and a long tail of things I built because I wanted to know whether they would work.

Studying Engineering Physics gave me hands-on grounding in software, mechanical, and electrical systems as one integrated skill set rather than three separate ones. That cross-disciplinary fluency is what lets me find solutions at the intersection of all three, where single-discipline engineers typically get stuck. The math- and physics-heavy coursework built a different asset: physical intuition sharp enough to reason naturally about how a robot perceives its environment and how it should act in response.

At the moment that means machine learning research on regulatory genomics at the de Boer Lab, and vision and estimation work for UBC Thunderbots — with regular detours into CAD, 3D printing, and whatever else the project needs. I would rather own a problem end to end than hand it across a boundary.

Outside of it I play music, play soccer, and am involved in more clubs than is strictly sensible. I value every genuine connection I make along the way.
